# Yuki Revised Art Direction: Calm Pixel-Fantasy

Yuki’s visual world should feel like a calm pixel-fantasy game discovered through a carefully made Pinterest moodboard: atmospheric, tactile, slightly strange, and emotionally spacious. It can become darker, duskier, rainier, or more mysterious at times, but it should not become frightening, loud, or overly cute.

The environment remains the primary character. The user enters a world with rooms, thresholds, weather, plants, small objects, distant lights, and hidden paths. Yuki is a quiet inhabitant who may sit, read, wander, observe the garden, or be absent from the immediate frame. Yuki must not look like a conventional mascot, human, robot, cat, anime character, or command-ready assistant.

The visual language combines soft pixel-art structure with painterly atmosphere. Pixel edges should be intentional and readable rather than mechanically low-resolution. Use layered parallax, subtle dithering, hand-placed highlights, and small animated details. The mood should remain calm even when the palette turns dark: deep moss, ink blue, plum, smoky lavender, muted teal, warm window gold, and tiny rose or amber accents.

The composition should support a phone-first portrait crop while retaining a side-on storybook perspective. The foreground may hold a small table, sketchbook, tea, plant, or pool; the middle ground holds the threshold and Yuki or an empty place where Yuki was; the background holds a window, dusk sky, garden silhouettes, distant lights, or a path that suggests more world beyond the frame.

Pinterest-inspired charm should come from curation and material contrast: taped paper fragments, tiny botanical marks, hand-drawn stars, found-object arrangements, color swatches, and quiet visual surprises. Do not turn the scene into a sticker collage, children’s cartoon, or decorative clutter. Cuteness is a small accent, never the main identity.

Atmosphere must be independently controllable from the base scene. Keep background, objects, Yuki, lighting, weather, particles, and mood overlays as reusable layers or components. The world should be able to move from soft morning to rainy afternoon, deep evening, or low-stimulation stillness without switching to a disconnected screen.

The first visual proof must remain standalone. It should demonstrate the art direction, scene composition, Yuki’s ambiguous silhouette, and the possibility of layered adaptation. It must not be integrated into the web app until the user explicitly approves alignment.
